Section 6.1 Pixel-Level Chromatic Indices

1. Spectral Stress Transformation

Before vectorization, chlorosis (yellowing) and necrotic spotting are amplified using the Excess Green Index ($ExG$) and visible-spectrum pseudo-NDVI ($NDVI_{VIS}$) derived from normalized RGB channels:

$$ExG(x,y) = 2G(x,y) - R(x,y) - B(x,y)$$ $$NDVI_{VIS}(x,y) = \frac{G(x,y) - R(x,y)}{G(x,y) + R(x,y) + \epsilon}$$
Live Simulator Evaluation: NDVI_VIS = 0.41 | ExG = +0.18
Section 6.2 Linear Algebra & Eigen-Decomposition

2. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Given $N=2,560$ training leaf vectors $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{49152}$, the mean vector $\mu$ and covariance matrix $C$ are decomposed into orthogonal principal axes ordered by variance ($\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_k$):

$$\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i, \quad C = \frac{1}{N} A^T A, \quad C v_i = \lambda_i v_i$$ $$z = U_k^T (x - \mu) \in \mathbb{R}^k$$
Live Subspace Projection: k = 40 axes (98.2% variance retained)
Section 6.3 Multivariate Logistic Mapping

3. Probabilistic Disease Classification

Combining compressed eigen-features $z \in \mathbb{R}^k$ with auxiliary microclimatic telemetry $e = [T, H]^T$, early infection probability $P(y=1 \mid z, e)$ is computed via L2-regularized logistic regression:

$$P(y=1 \mid z, e) = \sigma(w^T z + \theta^T e + b) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-(w^T z + \theta^T e + b)\right)}$$
Live Posterior Probability $p_0$: P(y=1 | z, e) = 0.942 (94.2%)
Section 6.4 Coupled Ordinary Differential Equations

4. Weather-Coupled SIR Outbreak Model

When $p_0$ exceeds alert threshold $\tau$, field-wide epidemic dynamics over time $t$ (days) follow coupled ODEs driven by a Gaussian-logistic temperature-humidity transmission rate $\beta(T,H)$:

$$\frac{dS}{dt} = -\beta(T,H)S(t)I(t), \quad \frac{dI}{dt} = \beta(T,H)S(t)I(t) - \gamma I(t), \quad \frac{dR}{dt} = \gamma I(t)$$ $$\beta(T,H) = \beta_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(T - T_{opt})^2}{2\sigma_T^2}\right) \cdot \left[\frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\alpha(H - H_{crit}))}\right]$$
Live Transmission Rate ($T_{opt}=26^\circ\text{C}, H_{crit}=75\%$): β(28.5°C, 84.0%) = 0.342 day⁻¹

Section 5.2: Core Modeling Assumptions

1. Uniform Spatial Resolution

All input leaf images are standardized and resized to fixed dimensions ($128 \times 128$ pixels) prior to RGB channel flattening.

2. Controlled Illumination Normalization

Gaussian denoising and histogram equalization are applied during preprocessing to suppress outdoor shadow artifacts.

3. Closed Field Population Conservation

Total crop foliage density in a monitored agricultural plot remains constant across a single growing cycle: $S(t) + I(t) + R(t) = 1$.

4. Nonlinear Microclimatic Coupling

Pathogen spore germination accelerates nonlinearly when relative humidity exceeds $H_{crit} = 75\%$ within optimal thermal bounds ($20^\circ\text{C} - 30^\circ\text{C}$).

1. Abstract

Crop diseases cause major agricultural yield losses worldwide, making early detection and outbreak forecasting critical for sustainable farming. Traditional manual inspection of crop leaves is slow, subjective, and often detects infections only after visible tissue damage has spread. This paper presents an AI-based mathematical framework that combines linear algebra, multivariate statistics, and differential equations to detect leaf diseases at an early stage and predict regional outbreak risk. Leaf images are converted into high-dimensional color and spectral vectors, transformed using vegetation indices such as the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index ($NDVI$), and compressed via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) using covariance matrices, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors. Early disease classification is performed using regularized logistic regression and nearest-neighbour distance metrics in the reduced feature space, while temporal disease spread under varying temperature and humidity is modeled using a modified Susceptible-Infected-Removed (SIR) differential equation system. Evaluated on benchmark leaf datasets using Python, NumPy, OpenCV, and Scikit-Learn, the framework achieves high classification accuracy (93.8%) while maintaining low computational complexity.

2. Introduction, Problem & Objectives

Plant pathogens—including fungi, bacteria, and viruses—destroy an estimated 20% to 40% of global crop production annually. Early foliar symptoms appear as subtle shifts in chlorophyll concentration and cellular reflectance before necrotic lesions become obvious to the human eye.

Problem Statement: Raw leaf images contain high-dimensional pixel data burdened by lighting variations, while standalone image classifiers ignore environmental weather conditions that drive pathogen spread.
  • Formulate pixel-level spectral transformations ($ExG, NDVI_{VIS}$) for pre-symptomatic leaf stress.
  • Apply PCA via covariance matrices, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors for dimensionality reduction.
  • Build an interpretable probabilistic classifier via L2-regularized logistic regression.
  • Model temporal crop disease propagation using a weather-coupled SIR differential equation system.

8.2 Limitations & 9. Conclusion

Limitations & Future Scope: While mathematically transparent and computationally lightweight, the current PCA visual pipeline assumes uniform leaf orientation and controlled lighting. Severe occlusion, overlapping leaves, or unmodeled soil nutrient deficiencies can reduce accuracy. Future extensions will integrate automated leaf segmentation (U-Net masks) and real-time meteorological APIs.

Conclusion: Unifying vector spaces, covariance eigen-decomposition ($93.8\%$ accuracy at $k=40$), logistic probability mapping, and weather-driven SIR differential equations provides both accurate single-leaf diagnosis and actionable temporal forecasting (preventing $>75\%$ canopy loss).
